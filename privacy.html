    1. Who we are

    MyScreenFolio (“MSF”, “we”, “us”) is a free website and installable web app for tracking, rating and sharing the movies, TV shows, anime and cartoons you watch. It is made and run by its two creators, Mohammad and Narimaan, university students based in the United Arab Emirates. MSF is not a company and doesn’t make money.

    For the purposes of data protection law, the creators are the controller of the personal data described here: we decide why and how it is used. You can reach us at myscreenfolio@gmail.com about anything in this policy.

    This policy is written to meet the UAE’s Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 on the Protection of Personal Data (the “PDPL”). Because people use MSF from all over the world, we also give everyone the rights that the EU and UK General Data Protection Regulations (GDPR) give — wherever you live, you can use the rights in section 10.

    2. What this policy covers

    It covers the MSF website, the app you can install from it (“Add to Home Screen”), and every feature in them — your library, ratings, Diary, lists, profile, Discover, social features, Messages, notifications, imports and exports, and Help & Guides. It also covers emails we send you and emails you send us.

    It doesn’t cover other websites you open from MSF — for example a trailer on YouTube, a person’s page on IMDb, a streaming service from “Where to watch”, or our own accounts on X, Instagram, TikTok and Telegram. They have their own privacy policies.

    This policy works together with our Terms of Service and our Cookie Policy, which explains every cookie and piece of browser storage MSF uses.

    3.2 Your profile

    Everything you choose to add: profile photo (or a poster as your photo), bio, quote, backdrop, Top 5 favourite titles, people, seasons, episodes and characters, Showcase shelves, medals and achievements you’ve earned, and profile settings such as your language, time zone, week start and Home layout. Your photo, bio and quote are optional. For a photo you upload we keep the whole picture you chose (made smaller) as well as the two framed copies made from it — the poster on your profile and the circle shown next to your name — so you can change the framing later without uploading again. Removing your photo deletes all three.

    Profile photos are checked on your own device before they’re uploaded: a small image-safety model runs in your browser and refuses pictures that look sexual. The picture itself is never sent to us for this check. If a picture is refused, we record that a picture was refused (not the picture) so admins can spot repeated attempts.

    3.3 Your library and ratings

    The titles you add and everything about them: status (Watchlist, Watching, Watched…), the priority you give a Watchlist title (Must Watch, Want to Watch or Eventually — saved with the title like its status; MSF only ever shows it to you), category, progress through seasons and episodes, scores and the MSF rating breakdown, episode and season ratings, rewatches, the dates you watched (your Diary), notes and spoiler marks, pinned notes, lists you make or join, posters you upload yourself, and which import a title came from. When you delete a title we note its id and the time for 90 days, so your other devices can remove it too. We also keep a short list of titles you’ve recently opened, the titles you’ve marked Not interested so they’re never recommended to you again, and how you’ve tuned your recommendations — titles you asked for More like this or Less like this, and the categories you turned off under Recommend from. Only you can see any of these (change them in Settings › Appearance & Preferences › Recommendations).

    Why: this is MSF — it’s what lets us show your library, your stats, your Year in Review, your taste profile and recommendations “For your taste”. Recommendations and taste matches are worked out from your own library, ratings and favourites (including your favourite actors and directors) and the choices you made to tune them, in your browser; we don’t profile you for anything else.

    3.4 Social features

    Who you follow and who follows you, follow requests, friends, people you block, comments and replies, likes and reactions, recommendations you send, polls and votes, Movie Nights, Challenges and teams, Episode Chat messages, shares to a friend’s Inbox, and the notifications these create. If you turn on Online status, friends see you’re online while you have their chat open.

    Why: to run those features, and to let people control them — for example, a block stops someone finding, following or messaging you, and is never told to them.

    3.5 Messages

    Messages between friends are end-to-end encrypted: they’re locked on the sender’s device and only unlocked on the recipient’s. Our servers only ever hold scrambled text that we can’t read. To make this work we store:

    • your public key (shared with friends so they can send you messages),
    • your private key, locked with a key made from your password on your device (we can’t unlock it), and — only if you make one — a copy locked with your recovery code (the code itself never leaves your device),
    • the scrambled messages and reactions, who they’re between, when they were sent, opened and starred,
    • if you set a Message Lock passcode, only a slow one-way hash of it.

    Unopened messages are deleted after 30 days, and opened ones at the end of the 7th day after they were opened (starred messages stay until nobody stars them). Your devices keep your unlocked key in the browser’s own database (IndexedDB) so you don’t have to unlock it every time; signing out removes it.

    Note. Because we can’t read messages, we can’t recover them if you lose both your password and your recovery code, and we can’t check them for rule-breaking unless someone shows us a message they received.

    3.6 Safety and moderation

    • Reports. When you report someone or something, we store who reported, what, the reason, your optional note and a copy of the reported text at that moment (so it can be judged even if it’s edited or deleted). The person reported is never told who reported them.
    • Automatic word filter. Text you save is checked against a list of offensive words. When the filter changes something, or a username is refused, we log a “flag” with the original text, so admins can review it.
    • Moderation records. Warnings, removals, suspensions and bans, with the reason and (for suspensions) the end date.
    • Rate limits. To stop spam we record that an action happened (your account ID, the kind of action and the time — nothing else). These records are deleted after one day.

    MSF’s admins are the creators. They can see what’s needed to keep MSF safe: reports, flags, public content, and a member’s account details (username, email, account dates, age group, library and social counts). They can’t see your password, date of birth or messages.

    3.7 Devices, sign-ins and technical data

    • Signed-in devices. Our sign-in provider (Supabase) keeps a record of each signed-in session: when it started, when it was last used, a description of the browser and device (the “user agent”, e.g. “Safari on iPhone”) and the IP address it signed in from. You can see your devices — without IP addresses, which we never send to the website — and sign any of them out in Settings › Security › Signed-in devices.
    • Sign-in protection. When you sign in on MSF, we note each try: which account, whether the password was right, the time, and a scrambled code made from the IP address (never the address itself — the code can’t be turned back into it). This lets us stop anyone guessing passwords — after too many wrong tries, their connection has to wait, not yours. We also keep the scrambled codes of the connections each account has signed in from, so your own connections still get in while someone else is being blocked, and so we can tell you about a sign-in from somewhere new.
    • Sign-in alerts. If someone gets your password wrong 5 times, or your account is signed in from a new connection, you get a notification with the device and browser it came from (e.g. “Windows PC · Chrome”). These alerts can’t be switched off. Choosing Not me signs that device out.
    • Server logs. Like every website, the services that host MSF (see section 6) automatically log requests: your IP address, the page or file asked for, the time, and your browser’s user agent. They use these logs for security, fixing faults and stopping abuse. We don’t combine them with your account.
    • Our TMDB helper counts requests per IP address, for a minute at a time and only in memory, to stop anyone abusing it. It isn’t stored.
    • Errors. MSF doesn’t send crash reports or error logs anywhere. If something breaks, it stays in your browser.

    3.9 Imports and exports

    When you import your history (from Letterboxd, IMDb, MyAnimeList, AniList, a spreadsheet, a text or Word file, or a pasted list), the file is read on your device; only the titles it finds are sent to your account. For AniList you give a username, and your browser asks AniList for that user’s public list. When you export, the file is made on your device and saved where you choose — we don’t keep a copy.

    3.10 Emails

    We send only the emails an account needs: confirming your address, resetting your password, and — rarely — telling you about an important change to your account or to these policies. No newsletters or marketing. When you email us, we keep the conversation for as long as we need to answer and follow up.

    3.11 What we don’t collect

    • No advertising, analytics, “pixels”, fingerprinting or social-media trackers.
    • No precise location. (Your IP address can show your rough area; we don’t use it for that.)
    • No contacts, camera, microphone, photos library access beyond the single picture you choose to upload.
    • No payment details — MSF is free.
    • No special categories of data (such as health, religion or beliefs) on purpose. Please don’t put sensitive information about yourself or others in public notes, bios or comments.

    4. Our legal bases

    Data protection law only lets us use personal data when we have a valid reason. Ours are:

    ReasonWhat it covers
    Providing the service you asked for (our contract with you)Your account, library, ratings, profile, social features, messages, notifications, imports and exports — everything in sections 3.1–3.5 and 3.9–3.10. Without it MSF can’t work.
    Your consentOptional cookies and storage (“Remember my choices”, “Faster pages”, “Extra sources”), optional profile details such as a photo, bio or quote, and Online status. You can withdraw consent at any time; that doesn’t affect what happened before.
    Legitimate interestsKeeping MSF and its members safe: security logs, signed-in device records, rate limits, the word filter, the picture check, reports and moderation. We’ve balanced these against your rights; they’re limited to what’s needed and you can object (section 10).
    Legal obligationsThe age checks, keeping records we must keep, and answering lawful requests from authorities.

    Under the PDPL these correspond to processing that is necessary to perform a contract with you, processing with your consent, processing necessary to protect the rights and safety of members, and processing needed to meet legal obligations.

    5. Who can see your things

    • Public account (the default): anyone, including people who aren’t signed in, can see your profile and whatever in your folio you’ve set to Anyone.
    • Private account: only followers you approve can see your library, Diary, lists and notes. Your username, display name, photo, bio and “Member since” are still visible, so people can find you and ask to follow.
    • Per item: each title, list and note can be Anyone, Friends or Only me. Only me means only you. A title set to Only me never notifies anyone, and narrowing who can see a title takes back, straight away, the notifications people already got about it if they can no longer see it.
    • Your Diary is on your profile as a Diary tab (its Timeline): the day you watched each title or episode (and the time, if one was saved), finishes, rewatches and your score at the time. Each watch follows its title — people only see watches of titles they’re allowed to see (Anyone, or Friends for friends; never Only me), and a private account’s Diary only to approved followers. Watches without a day, and anything you removed from your library, aren’t shown. Nobody else can change it.
    • Followers & following lists can be Everyone, Friends or Only me (Settings › Privacy).
    • Comments, replies, polls, Episode Chat and challenge activity you post on shared spaces are seen by the people who can see that space.
    • Friends’ activity: what you rate, add or finish can appear in your followers’ feeds and notifications. Settings › Notifications › What you share decides who hears about what.
    • Some settings are readable by other members’ apps when a feature needs them — for example your time zone, so a message you open disappears at the right time.

    Public profiles can be opened by anyone with the link and may appear in search engines. Anything you make public can be seen, copied or screenshotted by others — think before you post.

    7. Where your data is kept

    MSF’s providers run their servers outside the UAE — mainly in the United States and the European Union. That means your data is transferred abroad when you use MSF. We choose providers with strong security and data-protection commitments (including standard contractual protections for international transfers), and use them only to run MSF, as the PDPL requires for cross-border transfers.

    9. Deleting your account

    You can delete your account at any time in Settings › Account › Delete account (you’ll be asked for your password). It happens straight away and can’t be undone: your profile, library, ratings, Diary, lists, notes, comments, replies, reactions, follows, friendships, notifications, messages, Message Lock, keys and profile photo are deleted from our database.

    • Posters you uploaded are stored by Cloudinary. Remove them first in Settings › Data › My uploads, or email us and we’ll remove them for you.
    • Things others hold — a screenshot, a copy inside a report someone made about your content, or a list a friend copied — aren’t part of your account.
    • Backups held by our providers are overwritten on their normal schedule.

    Want a copy first? Settings › Data › Export and backup makes a full backup you can keep.

    10. Your rights

    Wherever you live, you can:

    • See your data — most of it is on your profile and in Settings; a full backup is in Settings › Data › Export and backup. You can also ask us for a copy of everything we hold about you.
    • Correct it — edit it in Settings, or ask us (for example, your date of birth).
    • Delete it — delete things one by one, delete your account, or ask us.
    • Take it with you — exports come as a spreadsheet, a Letterboxd file, a PDF or a full backup.
    • Withdraw consent — in Cookie settings, or by switching off what you turned on.
    • Object or ask us to limit how we use your data, including for safety checks based on legitimate interests.
    • Not be judged only by a machine — the word filter and picture check are automatic; if one affected you, you can ask a person to look again. MSF makes no automated decisions with legal or similarly serious effects.
    • Complain — to us first, please; and you can always complain to the UAE Data Office or to the data protection authority where you live.

    How: email myscreenfolio@gmail.com from the address on your account, saying what you’d like. We may ask you to confirm it’s you. We’ll reply within 30 days, and it’s free.

    11. Children and teens

    • You must be 13 or older to make an account. If a date of birth under 13 is entered, sign-up is refused and blocked on that device for a day; an existing account found to be under 13 is locked.
    • 13–17: please use MSF with a parent’s or guardian’s permission. Titles rated 16+ or 18+ are hidden according to your age group, and Messages are for members 16 and older.
    • Parents and guardians: if you think a child under 13 has an account, or you want your teen’s data deleted, email us and we’ll act quickly.

    12. How we protect your data

    • Everything travels over encrypted connections (HTTPS).
    • Database rules check every request, so each member can only read and change what they’re allowed to.
    • Passwords are hashed by Supabase; messages are end-to-end encrypted; the TMDB and Cloudinary keys stay on the server, never in your browser.
    • Too many wrong passwords block whoever is guessing (not you), and you’re told about wrong passwords and sign-ins from new connections.
    • You can see and sign out your devices, and set a Message Lock.
    • The site only runs its own code and talks only to the services listed above (a “Content Security Policy”).

    No system is perfectly secure. Use a strong password you don’t use elsewhere. If a breach ever puts your data at risk, we’ll tell you and the relevant authority as the law requires, without undue delay.
